About

Making quantum hardware something you can hold.

I'm a quantum computing researcher in the Searles Lab at the University of Illinois Chicago. My work centers on a conviction: the fastest way to grow the quantum workforce is to put real quantum hardware in students' hands. To that end, I design and build low-cost nuclear magnetic resonance quantum computers — complete single-qubit systems with custom control electronics, inexpensive enough for an undergraduate teaching lab.

On the other side of the bench, I study what today's noisy quantum processors can actually deliver. With collaborators across the IBM HBCU Quantum Center, I develop hardware-aware benchmarking workflows built on the Quantum Permutation Algorithm, run across multiple quantum architectures including IBM superconducting hardware. I also work on superconducting qubit simulation in Ansys HFSS and qubit control with the QICK platform, in collaboration with Fermilab SQMS-related research.

Before and alongside the Ph.D., I've spent a decade as an engineer at the Illinois Department of Transportation — running materials laboratories, supervising QA testing for $400M+ in infrastructure contracts, and applying machine learning to engineering problems. I hold licenses as a Professional Engineer in Illinois and Wyoming, plus PMP and CMP certifications. In 2025 I co-founded SpinQuorum to bring affordable quantum devices from the lab bench to classrooms.

Research

Four threads, one instrument panel.

Low-cost quantum devices

NMR quantum computing for the classroom

Design and development of control electronics for a low-cost single-qubit NMR quantum computer, plus compact portable spectrometers and optically detectable magnetic resonance devices for undergraduate education labs.

Benchmarking & characterization

What can NISQ hardware really do?

Hardware-aware performance evaluation of the Quantum Permutation Algorithm across quantum architectures, including case studies on IBM Sherbrooke — turning algorithm runs into honest measurements of near-term machines.

Superconducting qubits

Simulation and control

Electromagnetic simulation of superconducting qubits in Ansys HFSS, qubit control software development connected to Fermilab SQMS research, variable pulse timing and qubit cloaking on QICK control hardware.

Workforce development

Growing who gets to do quantum

Closing the quantum workforce gap through accessible instrumentation, mentorship of undergraduate researchers, and SpinQuorum — a startup building a desktop NMR quantum computing platform for education.
